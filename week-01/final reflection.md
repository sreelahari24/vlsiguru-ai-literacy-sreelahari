Week 01 — Final Reflection

1. What is the most useful thing I learned this week?
The most useful thing I learned this week is the difference between Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), Deep Learning (DL), and Generative AI. I understood that AI is not limited to generating text or images; it can also help with prediction, data analysis, optimization, and engineering tasks. I also learned how AI can support VLSI Physical Design, particularly in Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) optimization, placement, routing, and timing analysis.

2. What did I initially misunderstand?
Initially, I thought that every system that performs an intelligent task must use AI. I learned that traditional rule-based software can also perform useful tasks without machine learning. I also understood that AI-generated answers may sound convincing but can still contain incorrect or incomplete information. Therefore, it is important to understand how a system works and verify its results instead of relying only on its output.

3. What did AI help me do?
AI helped me understand technical concepts in simple language, organise my assignment answers, and explore practical applications of AI in VLSI Physical Design. It also helped me understand basic Python concepts such as variables, data types, input/output, functions, and debugging. I explored Python programs for calculating electrical power and converting frequency between Hz, kHz, and MHz, which helped me connect programming concepts with electronics.

4. Where did I need to verify or correct AI?
I learned that AI-generated information should be checked using reliable sources, independent reasoning, and practical testing. For example, an AI-generated Python program must be executed to confirm that it produces the expected output. Similarly, an AI-generated recommendation for Physical Design optimization must be evaluated using actual implementation results and timing reports. This helped me understand that a confident explanation is not sufficient evidence of correctness.

5. What is one question I want answered on Saturday?
How can AI and Machine Learning be practically used in VLSI Physical Design to improve PPA optimization, placement, routing, and timing closure, and how can engineers verify that the suggested optimizations actually improve the final design?

Overall Learning
Week 01 helped me build a foundation in AI literacy and understand the importance of using AI responsibly. I learned to ask questions, investigate technical claims, explore practical examples, and think critically about AI-generated results. I also began exploring Python as a useful skill for engineering automation and future AI applications.
My main takeaway is that AI should be used as an engineering assistant rather than blindly trusted. As a student interested in VLSI Physical Design, I want to develop the ability to combine AI tools with engineering knowledge, verify technical results, and take responsibility for my final decisions.
