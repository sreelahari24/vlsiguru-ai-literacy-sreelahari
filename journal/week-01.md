# Week 01 Learning Journal
What I learned:
This week I learned the basic concepts of AI and how AI systems are different from traditional software. I learned the relationship between AI, machine learning, deep learning, generative AI and AI agents. I also learned how LLMs process prompts using tokens and next-token prediction.
I learned that an AI-generated answer can sound correct but can still contain incorrect or unsupported information. Because of this, I should not automatically trust an AI response and should verify important information using reliable sources.
I also learned the difference between using an AI assistant, a web search engine, and an authoritative reference. An AI assistant can give a quick explanation, while an authoritative source is more useful for checking and verifying technical information.

What surprised me:
I was surprised that something can appear intelligent without actually being AI. For example, a calculator or a program using a fixed temperature rule can produce useful results without learning from data.
I also found it interesting that modern language models generate responses by predicting tokens step by step. This helped me understand why an AI response can be fluent while still being incorrect.

What I found difficult:
The difference between machine learning, generative AI, and AI agents was initially confusing. I understood it better after comparing definitions and examples from reliable sources.
The AI comparison and verification exercise also showed me that getting an answer is not enough. I need to check the answer against a reliable reference.

How my thinking changed:
Before this week, I mainly thought of AI as systems that give intelligent-looking answers. Now I understand that different AI systems can perform different tasks such as prediction, classification, generation, recommendation, and recognition.
I also understand that AI should be treated as an assistant rather than an unquestioned authority. Important results should be inspected and verified before they are used.

My verification habit:
My main learning from this week is:
Define the problem → Inspect the assumptions → Check the evidence → Test the result → Accept, reject, or revise.
I plan to use this process when working with AI for technical and engineering tasks.

AI Usage Declaration:
I used AI assistants to help me understand concepts, organize my answers, improve explanations, and review my work. I checked important claims against reliable sources and documented the verification process. The final answers were reviewed and adapted based on my understanding.
